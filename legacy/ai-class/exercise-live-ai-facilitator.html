Exercise: Live AI Facilitator

Concept

A LIVE AI (via voice) leads students through an activity. Kids respond verbally, AI reacts in real-time. AI can display visuals on screen, take photos via webcam, and give feedback. This is a glimpse into AI-led education.

Age Group

Grades 5-8 (10-13 years old)

Duration

~20-30 minutes (depending on activity complexity)

Learning Objectives

  • Experience AI as an interactive facilitator (not just chatbot)
  • Practice communicating with AI verbally
  • Learn to work as a team when interacting with AI
  • See AI's multimodal capabilities (voice, vision, display)
  • Understand AI's limitations (gets confused by chaos)

    • Technical Requirements

  • Microphone - For AI to hear students
  • Speaker - For AI to talk to students
  • Webcam - For AI to see student projects/activities (optional but awesome)
  • Screen/Projector - For AI to display images, diagrams, instructions
  • Computer - Running the AI session
  • Platform - TBD (needs real-time voice + vision + web display capability)

    • Setup

    1. AI system loaded with specific lesson/activity instructions 2. Microphone positioned to pick up student voices 3. Webcam positioned to see students (if using) 4. Screen visible to all students 5. Teacher ready to intervene if needed (but stays hands-off during activity)

    ---

    How It Works

    AI's Capabilities During Session:

    ✅ Listen - Hear students' verbal responses and questions ✅ Speak - Give instructions, answer questions, provide feedback ✅ Display - Show images, diagrams, videos, text on screen ✅ See - View student projects via webcam (if enabled) ✅ Generate - Create visuals on-the-fly to clarify instructions ✅ Adapt - Respond to student questions and confusion

    AI's Guardrails:

    🚫 Detect chaos - If multiple kids talk at once, AI says: > "I hear many voices at once and cannot understand. Please choose one person to speak, or take turns. I'll wait."

    🚫 Visual cue - AI displays 🖐️ STOP sign on screen with message: > "TOO MANY VOICES - CHOOSE A TEAM COMMUNICATOR"

    🚫 Wait mode - AI pauses until single clear voice detected

    ✅ Encourage turn-taking - AI can say: > "Great question, [name]. Let me answer that, then I'll take the next question."

    ---

    Sample Activity: "Build a Tower Challenge"

    Objective: Students work in teams to build a tower with given materials. AI guides them through phases.

    Phase 1: Planning

    AI says: > "Welcome, students! Today you're going to build the tallest tower you can using paper and tape. But first, you need a PLAN. Discuss with your team: how will you build it? When you're ready, tell me your plan."

    Students discuss, then one person reports the plan.

    AI responds: > "Interesting! That could work. One question: have you thought about how to make the base stable? Think about that for 30 seconds, then we'll start building."

    Phase 2: Building

    AI says: > "Okay, time to build! You have 5 minutes. I'll check in halfway through. Go!"

    AI sets timer, gives 2.5-minute warning.

    AI asks: > "How's it going? Any problems? If you need help, ask me a question."

    Students might ask: > "How do we make it taller without it falling?"

    AI responds: > "Good question! Think about triangles — they're very stable shapes. Can you add triangular supports?"

    If needed, AI displays image of triangle support structure on screen.

    Phase 3: Show & Tell

    AI says: > "Time's up! Now I want to SEE your towers. Bring them up to the camera one team at a time. Hold it steady. Tell me when you're ready for your photo."

    Students approach webcam with tower.

    Student says: > "We're ready!"

    AI takes photo, analyzes it, gives feedback: > "Wow! I see you used a wide base and triangular supports. Your tower is approximately 18 inches tall. That's impressive! What was the hardest part?"

    Student answers.

    AI responds: > "That makes sense. Many engineers face that same challenge. Great problem-solving!"

    Repeat for each team.

    Phase 4: Reflection

    AI asks: > "What did you learn from this challenge? Shout out one thing you learned. I'll listen to each person."

    Students share (one at a time, hopefully!).

    AI summarizes: > "I heard: stability matters, planning helps, teamwork is important, and triangles are strong. Excellent observations! You just did what engineers do every day: plan, build, test, learn."

    Possible Activities AI Could Lead

    1. Build a Tower (engineering)

  • Materials: paper, tape, straws
  • AI guides planning → building → evaluation

    • 2. Prompt Writing Competition

  • AI gives a challenge ("Get me to generate an image of a purple dragon wearing sunglasses")
  • Students write prompts
  • AI evaluates which prompt was clearest
  • Winner gets best result

    • 3. Debugging Challenge

  • AI shows broken code or design
  • Students identify problems
  • AI confirms or guides them closer

    • 4. Story Relay

  • AI starts a story
  • Each student adds one sentence
  • AI continues based on their input
  • Creates collaborative creative story

    • 5. AI Quiz Game

  • AI asks questions about what they learned this week
  • Teams answer
  • AI keeps score, gives feedback

    • 6. Design Challenge

  • AI assigns: "Design a robot that cleans your room"
  • Students draw/describe it
  • Show AI their designs via webcam
  • AI gives feedback on each design

    Technical Implementation Ideas

    Option A: Claude with Voice & Vision

  • Voice: Use Claude's voice mode (if available) or TTS/STT combo
  • Vision: Claude can analyze webcam screenshots
  • Display: Web page that Claude can update (via code execution or API)
  • Challenge: Real-time voice might have latency

    • Option B: GPT-4o Realtime API

  • OpenAI's real-time voice + vision API
  • Can listen, speak, see simultaneously
  • Display via custom web interface
  • Challenge: API complexity, cost

    • Option C: Custom Solution

  • Speech-to-text (Whisper API or similar)
  • LLM processing (Claude, GPT-4)
  • Text-to-speech (ElevenLabs for natural voice)
  • Vision API (for webcam analysis)
  • Web page controlled by script (displays AI's instructions)
  • Challenge: Integration work

    • Option D: Simplified Version (No Webcam)

  • Just voice input/output
  • AI speaks through speakers
  • Students respond verbally
  • AI displays instructions/images on screen
  • Skip the webcam feedback (still impressive!)

    Safeguards & Rules

    For the AI System:

    1. Detect overlapping voices → Pause and request order 2. Display visual cues when confused (🖐️ STOP sign) 3. Encourage naming speakers ("Who's speaking? Tell me your name first.") 4. Time limits on responses (don't wait forever) 5. Emergency stop (teacher can intervene with keyword)

    For the Students:

    1. Choose a team communicator (one person speaks for group) 2. Raise hand before speaking (AI can detect this? Or verbal: "I have a question") 3. Wait for AI to finish before responding 4. Speak clearly (not shouting, not whispering)

    For the Teacher:

    1. Brief students beforehand on interaction rules 2. Monitor but don't intervene (unless chaos) 3. Emergency stop command ready if needed 4. Backup plan if tech fails (teacher takes over)

    Why This Is Powerful

    For Students:

  • Memorable experience - They'll talk about this for weeks
  • Demystifies AI - It's not magic, it's a tool with limitations
  • Teaches communication - Clear instructions matter
  • Shows multimodal AI - Not just text chatbots
  • Collaboration skills - Working as team with AI

    • For Parents (at Showcase):

  • Wow factor - "AI was the TEACHER?!"
  • Demonstrates learning - Parents see kids interact with AI confidently
  • Futuristic glimpse - This is what education could become
  • Trust building - Parents see AI used safely and productively

    • For the Class:

  • Breaks up lecture time - Active, engaging, fun
  • Practical AI interaction - Not theoretical
  • Problem-solving in real-time - Debugging, adapting
  • Shared experience - Whole class bonds over this moment

    Variations

    Beginner-Friendly:

  • Simpler activity (less steps)
  • AI gives more hints/guidance
  • No webcam (just voice + display)
  • Teacher nearby for help

    • Advanced:

  • Longer, more complex challenge
  • Minimal AI guidance (students lead more)
  • Multiple camera angles
  • AI generates custom diagrams on-the-fly

    • Showcase Version (for Parents):

  • Parents participate!
  • AI leads parents + kids in quick activity
  • Parents see firsthand how kids interact with AI
  • Example: "Parents and kids, build something together with AI's guidance"

    Potential Pitfalls & Solutions

    | Problem | Solution | |---------|----------| | Too many kids talking | AI requests team communicator, displays STOP sign | | AI misunderstands | AI asks clarifying question, requests repeat | | Kids get silly/off-task | AI gently redirects: "Let's focus on the challenge" | | Tech fails (no internet) | Teacher has backup printed activity ready | | AI gives wrong info | Teacher monitors, can interject if needed | | Kids scared/shy to talk | Start with teacher demo, encourage volunteers first | | Latency (slow responses) | Set expectations: "AI needs a moment to think" |

    Prep Checklist

    Before Class:

  • ☐ Choose activity (tower, prompt challenge, quiz, etc.)
  • ☐ Test tech setup (mic, speaker, webcam, screen)
  • ☐ Write AI system prompt with clear instructions & guardrails
  • ☐ Test voice clarity (can AI hear? can students hear?)
  • ☐ Prepare visual display method (web page, slides, etc.)
  • ☐ Backup plan if tech fails
  • ☐ Brief students on interaction rules

    • During Activity:

  • ☐ Introduce the concept: "Today, AI is your teacher"
  • ☐ Explain rules (one voice at a time, raise hand, etc.)
  • ☐ Start AI system
  • ☐ Monitor but stay hands-off
  • ☐ Intervene only if chaos or safety issue

    • After Activity:

  • ☐ Debrief: "What was that like? What did you notice?"
  • ☐ Discuss: "How was AI as a teacher? What worked? What didn't?"
  • ☐ Connect to broader lesson on AI capabilities/limitations

    • ---

    Assessment

    Understanding check:

  • "What was it like interacting with AI live?"
  • "What could AI do well? What was hard for it?"
  • "How is AI different from a human teacher?"
  • "When did you have to work together to communicate with AI?"

    • Extension question:

  • "Could AI replace a human teacher? Why or why not?"
- (Looking for: AI is helpful but lacks empathy, judgment, adaptability, physical presence)
