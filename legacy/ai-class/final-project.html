Final Project: Two-Part Showcase

Overview

Students create TWO things for Friday's parent showcase: 1. AI-Generated Presentation - About what they learned (meta!) 2. Custom Web App - Solves a problem they care about

Both demonstrate AI literacy: using AI as a tool to amplify their own work.

---

Part 1: AI-Generated Presentation

The Process (Built Throughout the Week)

#### Daily Learning Capture When: Twice per day (before snack break + end of day) Duration: 5-10 minutes each time How: Students dictate/write what they learned so far

Before snack break (~1.5 hours into class): > "Okay everyone, before snack, let's capture what we've learned this morning. I'm going to ask: 'What did you learn today?' and we'll go around the room. You can say one thing or multiple things. I'll record it."

Teacher records (voice memo or typed notes):

  • Student names + what they said
  • Or: Students write on paper/digital doc
  • Or: Voice recording transcribed later

    • End of day (last 10 min): > "Before we wrap up, let's add to our learning log. What else did you learn this afternoon? What surprised you? What was hardest?"

    Capture daily for Monday-Thursday.

    ---

    Friday: AI Transforms Notes into Presentation

    Morning (first hour):

    Step 1: Compile All Notes

  • Gather all daily learning captures (4 days × 2 sessions = 8 chunks)
  • Create one big document with everything students said

    • Step 2: Feed to AI Students work individually or in pairs:

    Prompt engineering lesson!

    > "Now you're going to use AI to turn your notes into a presentation. Here's what you need to tell the AI: > > - What you want (a presentation about what you learned) > - Who the audience is (your parents) > - What style you want (informative, fun, impressive) > - What format (slides? paragraphs? bullet points?) > > This is where your prompting skills matter!"

    Example student prompt: > "I attended an AI summer class this week. Here are my notes about what I learned each day: [paste notes]. Please create a 5-minute presentation for my parents that explains what I learned in a way that's impressive and easy to understand. Organize it by day or by topic, whichever makes more sense. Make it sound like me (a 6th grader), not too formal."

    Step 3: AI Generates Draft

  • AI creates presentation outline
  • AI writes content for each section
  • Students review and customize

    • Step 4: Add Visuals

  • Students identify where images would help
  • Use AI image generation (DALL-E, Midjourney, Nano Banana Pro, etc.)
  • Prompt AI to create diagrams, illustrations, icons

    • Example visual prompts:

  • "Create a simple diagram showing how a context window fills up"
  • "Generate an image of a friendly robot teaching kids"
  • "Make an infographic comparing human vs AI strengths"

    • Step 5: Build Slides

  • Use Google Slides, PowerPoint, or web-based tool
  • Insert AI-generated text + images
  • Customize colors, fonts, layout
  • Add transitions (if time)

    • Step 6: Practice

  • Students rehearse their 2-3 minute presentations
  • Peer feedback
  • Final tweaks

    • ---

    Why This Is Brilliant

    Skills demonstrated: ✅ Prompt engineering - Giving AI clear instructions ✅ Context setting - Explaining what you need ✅ Synthesis - Turning raw notes into polished content ✅ Visual communication - Using images to enhance message ✅ Critical thinking - Reviewing AI output, not blindly accepting ✅ Iteration - Refining prompts to get better results

    Meta learning:

  • Students use AI to present about learning AI
  • Parents see the ENTIRE process (notes → AI → presentation)
  • Demonstrates AI as tool, not cheating
  • Shows planning + execution workflow

    • Parent-friendly:

  • Parents understand the content (it's what their kid learned!)
  • Impressive technical execution
  • Clear demonstration of skills gained
  • Easy to explain: "I used AI to help organize my thoughts"

    Part 2: Custom Web App

    The Challenge

    Build a web app that solves a problem YOU care about.

    Constraints (TBD based on time/skill):

  • Must be web-based (Chromebook-compatible)
  • Must use AI in some way (chatbot, image gen, search, etc.)
  • Must solve a real problem or serve a real interest
  • Must deploy (live URL to share)

    • The Process (Spread Across Week)

    #### Day 1-2: Brainstorm & Plan Students identify:

  • What problem do I want to solve?
  • What interests me?
  • What would be useful/fun to build?

    • Examples:

  • "I always forget my homework → Homework reminder app"
  • "I love animals → Animal fact chatbot"
  • "I want to learn Spanish → Vocabulary quiz game"
  • "I'm into space → Planet explorer with AI facts"
  • "I hate deciding what to eat → Meal suggestion app"

    • Brainstorm WITH AI: Students practice prompting AI to help them brainstorm:

    > "I want to build a web app but I'm not sure what. I like [interests]. What are some simple app ideas I could build in a few hours?"

    AI helps generate ideas, students choose one.

    ---

    #### Day 3: Planning & Design

    Students create planning documents:

    1. App description

  • What does it do?
  • Who is it for?
  • What problem does it solve?

    • 2. Features list

  • What can users do?
  • What pages/screens does it have?
  • What inputs/outputs?

    • 3. Technical plan

  • What AI model will I use? (ChatGPT API? Image generation?)
  • What will the interface look like?
  • What do I need to learn?

    • This is the VALUABLE part: planning before coding.

    Teacher emphasizes: > "Professional developers spend MORE time planning than coding. The better your plan, the easier coding will be. AI can only help you if you know what you want!"

    Document everything:

  • Sketch UI on paper
  • Write feature descriptions
  • List technical requirements

    • Feed plan to AI: > "I want to build [app description]. Here are my features: [list]. Here's a sketch of my interface: [describe]. Can you help me plan the technical structure? What HTML/CSS/JS will I need?"

    ---

    #### Day 4: Build & Debug

    Students use AI-assisted coding:

    Platform: Replit, Glitch, or similar (Chromebook-friendly)

    Workflow: 1. Start with template (teacher-provided or AI-generated) 2. Add features one at a time 3. Prompt AI for code when stuck 4. Test frequently 5. Debug with AI's help

    Example prompts: > "I want to add a text input box where users can type a question. Can you show me the HTML for that?"

    > "This button isn't working. Here's my code: [paste]. What's wrong?"

    > "How do I connect this form to the ChatGPT API so it sends the user's question and displays the answer?"

    Teacher circulates:

  • Help students debug
  • Guide prompt engineering
  • Ensure progress (scope down if needed)

    • Key lesson: > "It's okay if your app isn't perfect. Real developers iterate. What matters is that it WORKS and you understand what it does."

    ---

    #### Day 5 (Morning): Polish & Deploy

    Final touches:

  • Test all features
  • Fix bugs
  • Improve design (colors, fonts, layout)
  • Add title/instructions
  • Deploy (get live URL)

    • Deploy workflow:

  • Replit: One-click deploy, instant URL
  • Glitch: Auto-deploys, get shareable link
  • Students copy their URL to share with parents

    • Create app "demo script":

  • What does your app do?
  • What problem does it solve?
  • How do you use it?
  • What was hardest to build?
  • How did AI help you?

    • ---

    Why This Is Powerful

    Skills demonstrated: ✅ Problem identification - Finding real needs ✅ Planning - Thinking before building ✅ Communication with AI - Clear instructions = better code ✅ Context setting - Giving AI enough info to help ✅ Debugging - Finding and fixing errors ✅ Iteration - Testing, tweaking, improving ✅ Collaboration - Human + AI working together

    Cognitive development:

  • Planning skills (executive function)
  • Problem decomposition (breaking big task into small steps)
  • Logical thinking (if-then reasoning)
  • Resilience (debugging requires persistence)
  • Communication (explaining clearly to AI)

    • Parent-friendly:

  • Impressive visible result (working app!)
  • Personally meaningful (kid chose the problem)
  • Demonstrates independence (kid drove the project)
  • Clear AI role (tool, not magic)

    • ---

    Showcase Presentation Structure

    Each Student Gets ~3-4 Minutes

    Part 1: AI-Generated Presentation (1.5 min)

  • "Here's what I learned this week..."
  • Show slides
  • Explain: "I used AI to organize my notes into this presentation"

    • Part 2: Web App Demo (1.5 min)

  • "I built an app that [solves problem]"
  • Live demo (show it working!)
  • Explain: "AI helped me write the code, but I had to plan it and debug it"

    • Part 3: Q&A (30 sec)

  • Parents can ask quick question
  • Or: Gallery walk (parents explore apps at stations)

    • ---

    Logistics for Friday Showcase

    Option A: Individual Presentations

  • 20-30 kids × 3 min = 60-90 minutes (too long!)
  • Solution: Select 5-10 students to present, others do gallery walk

    • Option B: Gallery Walk

  • Students at stations with their apps open
  • Parents circulate, kids demo 1-on-1
  • Slides displayed on laptop or printed handout
  • Advantage: Everyone participates, more personal

    • Option C: Hybrid

  • Selected highlights present to whole group (3-5 kids)
  • Then gallery walk for everyone else
  • Advantage: Best of both (impressive demos + everyone included)

    Assessment Rubric (Optional)

    Presentation

  • ☐ Used AI to generate content from notes
  • ☐ Included visuals (AI-generated or sourced)
  • ☐ Organized and easy to follow
  • ☐ Demonstrated understanding of what they learned

    • Web App

  • ☐ Solves a real problem or serves an interest
  • ☐ Works (functional, deployed)
  • ☐ Uses AI in some way
  • ☐ Demonstrates planning (not just random coding)
  • ☐ Student can explain how it works

    • Process

  • ☐ Documented learning daily
  • ☐ Planned before building
  • ☐ Debugged and iterated
  • ☐ Used AI as a tool (not crutch)
  • ☐ Can articulate what AI helped with vs what they did

    Materials Needed

    For presentations:

  • ☐ Computers with internet (Google Slides or similar)
  • ☐ AI access (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)
  • ☐ Image generation tool access
  • ☐ Projector for showcase (or printed handouts)

    • For web apps:

  • ☐ Chromebooks (one per student)

  • ☐ Teacher-provided starter templates
  • ☐ Backup plan if APIs fail (local version)

Teacher Talking Points

When explaining the project: > "You're going to create TWO things this week. First, you'll use AI to turn your daily notes into an awesome presentation. Second, you'll build a real web app that solves a problem YOU care about. AI will help, but YOU are in charge. This is how professionals use AI: as a powerful tool that makes them faster and better."

During the showcase: > "These students didn't just learn ABOUT AI—they learned to USE it. They planned, built, debugged, and deployed real projects. The apps you see are 100% functional. The presentations were created WITH AI, not BY AI. These kids are the ones who told AI what to do. That's the skill that matters."
